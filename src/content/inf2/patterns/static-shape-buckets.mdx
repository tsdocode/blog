Serving traffic is dynamic: streams join and leave, contexts grow, prompts have different lengths. NKI kernels are
static (see <T id="programming/tracing-and-compilation">tracing and compilation</T>). The bridge is **buckets**: a
few static shapes, with each step padded up to the smallest one that fits.

## Designing the buckets

1. **Fix what you can.** The talker step always has 32 decode rows, one per slot; empty slots are padding with no
   visible keys. That removes the batch size from the shape entirely.
2. **Bucket what you cannot.** Two dimensions vary: context length (pages per stream) and how many new prompts
   arrive. Our talker uses 4 page buckets × 4 prefill buckets = **16 variants**.
3. **Mind the padding cost.** A padded page still costs its matmuls. With the smallest page bucket at 2 pages, half
   of all decode attention ran on empty pages for short utterances (contexts under 128 tokens). Adding a 1-page bucket
   took the talker step for 32 such streams from 18.8 to 16.5 ms.
4. **Mind hardware thresholds.** Prefill buckets of 1 / 2 / 3 tiles (≤ 416 tokens) keep the whole step under 512
   tokens; 4 tiles (544) made every weight block run a second, 32-token matmul.
5. **Compile them all at startup**, plus an end-to-end pass that exercises the eager ops around them.

| Dimension | Buckets | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| decode rows | 32 (fixed) | one per slot; padding rows fully masked |
| pages per stream | 1, 2, 4, 8 | 128 / 256 / 512 / 1024 tokens of context |
| prefill tiles | 0, 1, 2, 3 | 128-token tiles of packed prompts; T ≤ 512 |
| codec frames per call | 2 | chunked codec every 2 steps |

`PPS_BUCKETS = (1, 2, 4, 8)` and `PREFILL_TILE_BUCKETS = (1, 2, 3)`; `warmup()` compiles every combination, then runs
bursts of 1–20 real requests so the eager ops compile too.
