The Vector and Scalar engines cannot reduce *across* partitions. But a matmul contracts over the partition axis by
definition, so with a stationary operand of all ones:

```text
out[m, n] = Σ_p ones[p, m] · x[p, n] = Σ_p x[p, n]       for every m = 0..127
```

one `nc_matmul(stationary=ones[128, 128], moving=x[128, N])` writes the column sums of `x` into **all 128 output
partitions** — a reduction and a broadcast in one instruction, accumulated in PSUM if you need more than 128 rows.

## Where it shows up

- **RMSNorm over features on partitions.** Square, ones-matmul (accumulating over the feature tiles), then `rsqrt`
  — every partition ends up holding the same `1/rms` per token, ready for an element-wise multiply.
- **Per-head norms with block-diagonal ones.** With two 64-dim heads packed into one 128-partition tile, a
  block-diagonal matrix (two `64 × 64` blocks of ones) sums each head separately and broadcasts each head's sum over
  its own 64 partitions.
- **Attention scores without per-row matmuls.** Multiply `q ⊙ K` element-wise for all heads and slots at once, then
  one ones-matmul sums over the head dimension and broadcasts the score — this is how the short (≤ 16 slot)
  code-predictor attention works.

## Cost

The matmul is cheap — one Tensor Engine instruction instead of a long cross-partition shuffle — but it is a matmul:
on fp32 inputs it costs 4× bf16, and like every matmul it has a fixed overhead per call. Reduce wide tiles at once
rather than column by column.
