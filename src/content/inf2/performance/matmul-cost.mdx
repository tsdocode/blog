The <T id="hardware/tensor-engine">Tensor Engine</T> is a deep pipeline: what limits it is how often it can start a
new instruction, not the arithmetic inside one. I measured it directly — a kernel issuing 2,048 back-to-back
`nc_matmul`s with a `128 × 128` stationary tile and a moving tile of `N` columns:

| Moving tile | ns per matmul | Useful work |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 2 columns (one decode query, 2 heads) | 133 | 1.6% of a full matmul |
| 128 columns | 135 | 25% |
| 512 columns (one full PSUM bank) | 345 | 100% |

Two things stand out. Everything up to ~128 columns costs the same ~130 ns — that is the per-instruction floor in our
kernels (load the stationary tile, start the pipeline, drain into PSUM). And at 512 columns a matmul does 256× the
work of the 2-column one for 2.6× the time. The AWS cost model says the same thing in cycles: a matmul costs about
`max(N, 64)` array cycles, plus stationary loads that are only partly hidden.

## What it means

- **Count matmul instructions, not FLOPs.** Decode attention does tiny arithmetic in ~78,000 matmuls per step and
  costs ~8 ms; the whole dense path does 90 GFLOPs in far fewer, wider matmuls and costs 11 ms.
- **Make the moving operand wide.** Put the dimension with the most columns on the moving side; batch heads, pages or
  tokens into the same matmul when the stationary operand is shared.
- **Never leave a short tail.** A 544-token step ran every weight block as a 512-column matmul *plus* a 32-column one
  — 86,000 extra ~130 ns instructions, about 11 ms.
- **Transposes are matmuls too.** Each costs the same floor; avoid layouts that need one per page per head.
