Inferentia2 is the chip inside AWS's `inf2` instances. Think of it less as "a GPU from AWS" and more as two
independent processors that happen to share a package and a pool of memory: each half is a
<T id="hardware/neuroncore-v2">NeuronCore-v2</T>, with its own engines, its own on-chip memory and its own
instruction streams. A kernel runs on one NeuronCore; the chip's two cores never share SBUF.

## What is on the chip

- **Two NeuronCore-v2.** Each one is a full accelerator: a 128×128 <T id="hardware/tensor-engine">Tensor Engine</T>,
  <T id="hardware/vector-engine">Vector</T>, <T id="hardware/scalar-engine">Scalar</T> and
  <T id="hardware/gpsimd">GpSimd</T> engines, 24 MiB of <T id="hardware/sbuf">SBUF</T> and 2 MiB of
  <T id="hardware/psum">PSUM</T>.
- **HBM.** 32 GiB shared by both cores. Model weights, KV caches and every kernel's inputs and outputs live here.
- **DMA engines.** 32 of them move data between HBM and each core's SBUF, in parallel with compute.
- **Collective cores and NeuronLink-v2.** Hardware for AllReduce / AllGather between chips. A single-chip
  `inf2.xlarge` has no NeuronLink, so for one-chip serving you can ignore them.

The peak numbers are per chip: 380 INT8 TOPS, 190 TFLOPS for bf16 / fp16 / fp8 / tf32 and 47.5 TFLOPS for fp32.
On this generation fp8 runs at the same matmul rate as bf16 — it halves weight bytes, not compute time.

## The instance family

| Instance | Chips | vCPUs | Host RAM | Device memory |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| inf2.xlarge | 1 | 4 | 16 GiB | 32 GiB |
| inf2.8xlarge | 1 | 32 | 128 GiB | 32 GiB |
| inf2.24xlarge | 6 | 96 | 384 GiB | 192 GiB |
| inf2.48xlarge | 12 | 192 | 768 GiB | 384 GiB |

The `inf2.xlarge` is the interesting one for small-model serving: one chip, a tiny host (4 vCPUs, 16 GiB),
and a price in the same range as a single small GPU instance.

## Why it is underrated

The silicon is not small: 820 GB/s of HBM bandwidth and 190 dense bf16 TFLOPS per chip. What it lacks is
the CUDA ecosystem — there is no cuBLAS / FlashAttention / CUDA Graphs equivalent you can just import. You get a
compiler (good for standard transformer graphs) and <T id="programming/nki">NKI</T> for everything else, and the
rest of this guide is about what it takes to use the second one well.
