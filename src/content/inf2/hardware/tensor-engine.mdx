The Tensor Engine is where the FLOPs are. It is a **systolic array** of 128 × 128 processing elements (PEs).
Every PE does one multiply-accumulate per cycle at 2.8 GHz, so the array peaks at
128 × 128 × 2 FLOPs × 2.8 GHz ≈ **92 bf16 TFLOPS** per core.

## Stationary and moving

`nc_matmul(dst, stationary, moving)` computes `dst = stationaryᵀ @ moving`:

- **stationary** `[K, M]` — loaded into the array once; `K ≤ 128` (partitions), `M ≤ 128`.
- **moving** `[K, N]` — streamed through column by column; `N ≤ 512`.
- **dst** `[M, N]` — always fp32, written into one <T id="hardware/psum">PSUM</T> bank (2 KiB = 512 fp32 per partition).

The contraction dimension `K` must sit on the SBUF partition axis of both operands. This is why NKI kernels keep
activations **transposed** — `[features on partitions, tokens on the free axis]` — so a weight tile can be the
stationary operand and a block of tokens the moving one, with no reshuffling between layers.

Swapping the two operands gives you the transposed result. A transpose is literally a matmul against the
identity matrix — the Tensor Engine is the main way data crosses partitions.

## What it costs

The array is deeply pipelined, so what matters is how often it can start a new instruction:

- A matmul costs roughly **max(N, 64) array cycles** when the stationary tile is reused. Moving tiles narrower
  than 64 columns waste the array.
- Loading a new stationary tile is extra work, partly hidden by a background load during the previous matmul.
- fp32 inputs cost about 4×; bf16, fp16, fp8 and tf32 all run at the same rate (fp8 saves bytes, not time).

In practice, in our kernels a small matmul cost about **130 ns** no matter how small it was, and a full
`128 × 128 × 512` one about 345 ns. The best microbenchmark I ran reached about half of the 92 TFLOPS peak; that
benchmark accumulated every matmul into a single PSUM bank, and getting closer to peak needs back-to-back
`128 × 512` moving tiles across several banks. The lesson that shaped every kernel: **issue fewer, wider
matmuls** — see <T id="performance/matmul-cost">the matmul cost model</T>.
