An SBUF or PSUM tile is written `[P, F1, F2, ...]`. The first dimension is the **partition axis**: up to 128, one
per lane, processed in parallel. The rest are **free axes**, walked sequentially by each lane. An engine
instruction costs roughly the free-axis size in cycles, almost independent of how many partitions it uses.

## The rules that follow

1. **Put the big parallel dimension on partitions, and fill all 128.** A `[32, 4096]` op costs as much as a
   `[128, 4096]` one.
2. **Matmuls contract over the partition axis.** For `y = x @ Wᵀ` with the weight as the stationary operand, both
   operands need the input-feature dimension on partitions — so the activation tile is `[features, tokens]`.
3. **Element-wise ops pair partition `p` with partition `p`.** You can add two tiles, or a tile and a per-partition
   scalar `[P, 1]`, but not a row vector across partitions — broadcasting across partitions needs a matmul or a DMA.
4. **Reductions are cheap along the free axis and expensive across partitions.** A row max over the free axis is
   one Vector instruction; a sum over partitions is a <T id="patterns/ones-matmul">ones-matmul</T>.

## Transposed activations

Most of our kernels keep activations **transposed**: `xT [hidden, tokens]`, hidden dimension tiled over
partitions as `[128, hidden/128, tokens]`. Weights are the stationary operand; every matmul output
`[out_features, tokens]` lands in exactly the layout the next layer's matmul wants. A 28-layer transformer step runs
with no transposes in the dense path.

The exceptions are where an algorithm wants the other layout — softmax over keys wants keys or queries on the free
axis, top-k sampling wants each stream's vocabulary on the free axis — and there the kernel either transposes on the
<T id="hardware/tensor-engine">Tensor Engine</T> or swaps matmul operands so the result comes out the other way.
