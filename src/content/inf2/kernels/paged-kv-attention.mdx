Continuous batching means streams start and finish at different times, so each stream's KV cache cannot be one
contiguous buffer. As in vLLM's PagedAttention, the cache is a pool of fixed-size **pages** (128 tokens) and each
stream has a block table listing its pages.

## Layout: token-major

The cache is `[layer, block, 128 tokens, 8 KV heads, 128 dims]`. Two reasons for token-major:

- **Writes.** A new token's K for one layer is one contiguous 2 KiB row, and all of a step's tokens go out in one
  `vector_offset` scatter. A dimension-major K (ready for Q·Kᵀ) needed 128 tiny writes per token — 49 ms for a
  96-token step.
- **Reads.** A whole page is one contiguous region, gathered with one `scalar_offset` DMA (243 GB/s measured —
  about twice the per-partition `vector_select` alternative).

The price is that Q·Kᵀ needs K with the head dimension on partitions, so each page is transposed on the Tensor
Engine (a matmul with the identity) after it arrives.

## The attention itself

Per layer, per chunk of up to 4 streams, per KV head:

1. Transpose each page's K for this head: `[128 keys, 128 d] → [128 d, 128 keys]`.
2. Scores with **keys on partitions**: `S[keys, queries]`, with the transposed K page as the stationary operand
   and the queries moving — one matmul per page.
3. Add the causal mask (−30000 for keys past each query's position), max over keys (a free-axis reduce over pages,
   then one transpose for the partition axis), `exp`, sums via a ones-matmul.
4. `P · V` accumulated over pages in PSUM; V pages are already `[keys, d]`.

Pages for chunk `c + 1` are DMA'd into a second buffer while chunk `c` computes (ping-pong), and padding pages —
ids past the end of the whole cache — are skipped by the DMA entirely.

## Why it is the slowest part of the step

Decode has one query per stream: the moving operand of the Q·Kᵀ and P·V matmuls is 2 columns wide (two query heads
per KV head). Each of those matmuls, and each transpose, pays the Tensor Engine's fixed per-instruction cost.
With 32 streams, 3 pages each, 8 KV heads and 28 layers, the profile counted about **78,000 attention matmuls per
step**, and attention costs ~8 ms against a byte floor of ~3 ms. Fewer, wider matmuls — for example computing P·V for all 16 heads of a page in two
512-column matmuls — is the next optimisation. See <T id="performance/matmul-cost">the matmul cost model</T>.
