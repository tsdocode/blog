Before the audio-rate convolutions, the codec runs an 8-layer transformer over frames (hidden 512, 16 heads of 64,
sliding window of 72 frames). It is tiny — 59 MB of weights, 64 tokens per call for 32 streams × 2 frames — and it
was still the most expensive part of the codec. Its story is the clearest lesson in this guide about DMAs.

## The kernel

Two 64-dim heads share one 128-partition tile ("head pairs"); a block-diagonal <T id="patterns/ones-matmul">ones-matmul</T>
computes both heads' `q · k` and broadcasts each score over its head's 64 partitions, so attention needs no
transposes. Each stream keeps a K/V state of the last 71 frames per layer and head pair; each call appends this
chunk's frames and writes back the newest 71.

## 12.1 ms: it was not the math

The first fix attempted was obvious: the kernel issued about 8,200 small DMAs per call (per layer × head pair × stream
× K/V in/out), so the state layout was changed to keep all streams side by side and move each (layer, head pair) in
one DMA. **12.1 → 11.25 ms.** Barely anything.

The profile told the real story:

```text
device 11510.7 us | HBM rd 153.66 MB wr 74.58 MB | eff 13 GB/s read | DMA 94% TensorE 2% VectorE 27%
```

DMA 94% busy, moving data at 13 GB/s. The state was DMA'd straight into `kall[:, :, 0:R]` — a slice of a wider tile
that also holds the new frames — so every partition's destination was strided, and each transfer split into
128 × 32 = 4,096 descriptors of ~140 bytes instead of 128 large ones. Same bytes, 32× the descriptors.

## The fix

Load into a contiguous staging tile (one ~4.5 KiB descriptor per partition), then copy on chip — on-chip copies are
cheap:

```python
kst = nl.ndarray((P, B, R), dtype=nl.bfloat16, buffer=nl.sbuf)
nisa.dma_copy(dst=kst, src=k_state[l, p])                     # contiguous: fast
nisa.activation(dst=kall[:, :, 0:R], op=nl.copy, data=kst)    # on-chip, on the idle ScalarE
```

**11.6 → 4.0 ms.** The profile then showed the Vector Engine at 93% busy and the Scalar Engine at 8%, so the staging
copies moved to ScalarE (`activation(op=copy)`): **3.3 ms.** The whole codec call dropped from 36.7 to 27.9 ms —
about 4.4 ms off every engine step.

## The lesson

On a NeuronCore, a DMA's cost is its descriptor count, not its byte count. Profile before optimising: the first,
"obvious" fix (fewer DMAs) was the wrong one.
